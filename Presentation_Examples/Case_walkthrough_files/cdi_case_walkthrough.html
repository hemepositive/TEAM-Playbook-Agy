The Note That Cost $9,000

A total knee replacement patient develops acute kidney injury on postoperative day 2. The clinical picture is clear. The documentation isn't — and the DRG stays at 470.

The Case

A 71-year-old male undergoes elective right total knee arthroplasty for severe osteoarthritis. His history includes hypertension, type 2 diabetes, and CKD stage 3 (baseline creatinine 1.4). The procedure is uncomplicated. On POD 2, creatinine rises to 2.3. Urine output drops. Nephrology is consulted. IV fluids are given. By discharge the creatinine has trended down to 1.8.

The attending writes a discharge summary. Here's what it says.

Discharge Summary — As Written UNDERDOCUMENTED
Discharge Diagnoses: 1. Status post right total knee arthroplasty 2. Hypertension 3. Type 2 diabetes mellitus 4. Elevated creatinine, improving 5. Chronic kidney disease Hospital Course: Patient underwent uncomplicated right TKA. Postoperatively he did well. Creatinine was noted to be elevated on POD 2, nephrology was consulted, and patient was managed with IV fluids. Creatinine trended toward baseline prior to discharge. Patient is ambulating well and cleared for rehab.
⚠
Missed MCC "Elevated creatinine, improving" is not a billable diagnosis. It describes a lab value, not a clinical condition. The coder cannot assign N17.9 (Acute Kidney Failure) from this language — and without that code, there is no MCC. DRG stays at 470.
◎
Vague Phrasing in Hospital Course "Creatinine was noted to be elevated" describes an observation, not a diagnosis. Coders require the physician to name the condition. A nephrology consult and IV fluid treatment are strong clinical indicators — but attending documentation controls the DRG, not the consultant's note.
Discharge Summary — Revised COMPLETE
Discharge Diagnoses: 1. Status post right total knee arthroplasty 2. Acute kidney injury (AKI), resolving 3. Hypertension 4. Type 2 diabetes mellitus, with hyperglycemia 5. Chronic kidney disease, stage 3 Hospital Course: Patient underwent uncomplicated right TKA. On POD 2 the patient developed acute kidney injury in the setting of decreased oral intake and perioperative fluid shifts. Baseline creatinine 1.4, peak 2.3. Nephrology consulted; patient treated with IV fluid resuscitation with good response. Creatinine improved to 1.8 at discharge. Patient is ambulating and cleared for rehabilitation. The AKI is felt to represent a prerenal process superimposed on CKD stage 3, now resolving.
✓
MCC Captured "Acute kidney injury" is a named diagnosis the coder can map to N17.9. It appears in both the diagnosis list and the hospital course with clinical context (baseline, peak, treatment, resolution). This is codeable.
✓
Causality Established The final sentence establishes clinical causality and supports POA = Yes (present on admission as a POA-eligible perioperative condition). This protects against HAC scrutiny and supports medical necessity.
i
Bonus: Diabetes Specificity "Type 2 diabetes mellitus, with hyperglycemia" maps to E11.65 — a CC. It won't change this DRG (LEJR is 2-tier — only MCCs move the DRG) but it improves APR-DRG SOI, which affects quality metrics and commercial payer contracts.

DRG Impact

LEJR is a 2-tier DRG. The only thing that moves this case from MS-DRG 470 to MS-DRG 469 is a documented MCC. Here is what that single documentation change produces.

The clinical work was identical in both scenarios. The creatinine rose. Nephrology was consulted. IV fluids were given. The difference is four words: "acute kidney injury, resolving."

The Documentation Rule

Documentation Principle
A lab value is not a diagnosis. A treatment is not a diagnosis. Only a physician-documented clinical condition — named explicitly — can be coded. If you treated it, name it.

Coders work from physician documentation. They cannot infer a diagnosis from a lab trend, a consult, or a medication. The following language is not codeable as AKI:

✗
"Creatinine elevated, trending down" — describes a lab value only
✗
"Renal function worsened postoperatively" — vague, not a named condition
✗
"Nephrology consulted for elevated creatinine" — the consult is not the diagnosis
✗
"Possible AKI" — uncertain language ("possible," "probable," "suspected") is not coded as confirmed for inpatient stays unless it is a definitive discharge diagnosis, and even then clarity is preferable

The following language is codeable:

✓
"Acute kidney injury" — anywhere in the record with clinical context
✓
"AKI, prerenal, resolving" — specificity is a bonus; "AKI" alone is sufficient
✓
"Acute kidney failure" — clinical synonym accepted by CMS coding guidelines

Relevant Codes

For reference, the codes involved in this case:

N17.9 — AKI, unspecified · MCC N17.0 — AKI with tubular necrosis · MCC N18.3 — CKD Stage 3 · CC E11.65 — T2DM with hyperglycemia · CC

N17.x codes are MCCs only when used as secondary diagnoses with an appropriate principal diagnosis. POA indicators apply. CKD stage 3 (N18.3) is a CC but does not reach MCC — stage 5 (N18.5) is an MCC.

Before You Sign the Discharge Summary

For any LEJR patient with a creatinine bump postoperatively:

1
Did creatinine rise ≥ 0.3 mg/dL above baseline, or did urine output drop? Name it as AKI in your discharge diagnoses.
2
Was a nephrology consult placed? Confirm AKI is in your note — not just theirs. The attending's documentation governs DRG assignment.
3
Did it resolve before discharge? Document "AKI, resolving" or "AKI, resolved." Resolution does not eliminate the diagnosis — it was still present during the stay.
4
Is there underlying CKD? Document the stage. CKD stage 3–4 is a CC. CKD stage 5 is an MCC.
