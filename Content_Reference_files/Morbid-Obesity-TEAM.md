Morbid obesity (such as ICD-10 code E66.01) and having a Body Mass Index (BMI) of 40 or greater are generally classified as a **Complication or Comorbidity (CC)** rather than a Major Complication or Comorbidity (MCC).

Within the Transforming Episode Accountability Model (TEAM), the sources specifically highlight morbid or severe obesity as a relevant CC for the following surgical episodes:

- **Spinal Fusion:** Obesity (BMI ≥ 40) is a CC because it can complicate surgical procedures, affect positioning, increase intubation risks, and impact overall healing.
- **Major Bowel Procedures:** For colorectal surgeries, a BMI > 40 is considered an impactful CC because it increases surgical risks and influences perioperative management.
- **Lower Extremity Joint Replacement (LEJR) and Surgical Hip/Femur Fracture Treatment (SHFFT):** Severe obesity (BMI > 40) is a common CC in orthopedic procedures that can significantly affect a patient's care plan, treatment, and length of stay.

While morbid obesity is a universally recognized CC, the provided checklists for the fifth TEAM procedure, **Coronary Artery Bypass Graft (CABG)**, do not explicitly name it among their specific examples of common CCs or MCCs.

Beyond standard DRG assignment, it is also highly impactful to document **morbid obesity (E66.01, E66.2) because it is designated as a Hierarchical Condition Category (HCC 48)**. In the TEAM program, capturing HCCs correctly is critical because CMS uses them to calculate the beneficiary's risk profile, which ultimately increases the risk-adjusted target price for the 30-day episode of care..
