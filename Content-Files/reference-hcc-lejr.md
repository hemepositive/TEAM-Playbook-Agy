# HCC Reference: Lower Extremity Joint Replacement

HCCs documented in the 180 days before your patient's LEJR surgery directly raise the risk adjustment multiplier on their target price. A higher target price means the hospital has more room within the episode before it exceeds the benchmark. Underdocumented HCCs produce a lower target price than the patient's complexity warrants — making the episode appear more expensive relative to the benchmark than it actually is.

LEJR has 16 beneficiary-level HCC risk adjustment variables — more than any other TEAM procedure. This is partly because joint replacement is the highest-volume TEAM procedure and partly because CMS has the most outcome data on this population. Every HCC below was selected because it predicts higher episode spending in the LEJR population.

[PEARL] The 180-day lookback window ends one day before episode initiation. HCCs documented at any outpatient visit, pre-admission testing encounter, or prior inpatient stay within that window count. The surgical hospitalization itself does not open the HCC window — it closes it.

[NOTE] HCCs must be re-documented each calendar year to count toward current-year risk adjustment. A diagnosis carried forward from a prior year without active documentation — assessment, management plan, or clinical narrative — does not capture the HCC for the current period. The "history of" trap is the most common HCC documentation failure: chronic active conditions documented as "history of" are treated as resolved and do not count.

## Highest-Priority HCCs for LEJR Documentation

Not all HCCs carry equal weight in the LEJR risk adjustment model. Based on prevalence and impact in the joint replacement population, these warrant particular attention:

**Heart failure** (HCCs 224, 225, 226) — extremely common in the LEJR population and highly sensitive to documentation specificity. The difference between "CHF" (I50.9, lowest weight) and "chronic systolic heart failure" (I50.22, HCC 226) versus "acute on chronic systolic heart failure" (I50.23, HCC 224) represents meaningfully different risk weights. Specify the subtype and acuity at every encounter.

**Diabetes with complications** (HCC 37) — the majority of diabetic LEJR patients have at least one chronic complication. "Type 2 DM" without complication specification maps to HCC 19 (diabetes without complication) — a lower-weight category. Document the specific complication: neuropathy, nephropathy, retinopathy, or peripheral vascular disease.

**COPD** (HCC 280) — prevalent in the joint replacement population and frequently underdocumented. "Lung disease" or "pulmonary history" is not codeable. "COPD, moderate persistent, managed with [inhaler regimen]" is.

**Atrial fibrillation** (HCC 238) — among the most common cardiac comorbidities in the elective joint replacement population. "A-fib" codes correctly; "palpitations" or "irregular heartbeat" does not.

**Dementia** (HCCs 125, 126, 127) — relevant for the SHFFT overlap population and for elderly LEJR patients. The severity tier (mild/moderate/severe) affects which HCC is captured. A patient on Aricept who is described as "mildly confused" has documented dementia; the physician needs to name it and stage it.

## The Annual Re-Documentation Requirement

HCCs do not carry forward automatically. CMS requires that each HCC condition be documented — with an active assessment and management plan — within the current calendar year for it to count toward that year's risk adjustment.

For LEJR patients, this means pre-operative documentation is the primary HCC capture opportunity. The pre-admission history and physical, anesthesia pre-op visit, or any outpatient encounter in the 180 days before surgery is the window. A patient with well-documented heart failure, COPD, and diabetes whose HCCs were last documented 14 months ago has effectively zero HCC risk adjustment on their LEJR episode — even though the conditions are real, active, and expensive.

[PHRASING]
❌ "History of CHF — stable." — Codes as personal history; no HCC captured.
✅ "Chronic systolic heart failure, NYHA Class II, stable on current medical regimen including lisinopril and furosemide. Continued management." — HCC 226 captured; clinical management documented.
