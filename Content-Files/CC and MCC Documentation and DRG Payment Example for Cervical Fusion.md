### **Examples of Improved Documentation**

Here are specific examples showing the impact of precise documentation, transitioning a case from a lower to a higher-paying DRG.

#### **Case 1: The Diabetes Patient 🍬**

* **Before CDI:** The physician's note simply lists "Diabetes." This is not a CC or MCC, and the case stays in **MS-DRG 473** ($14,204.40).  
* **Improved Documentation:** The physician notes:  
  * "**Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus with peripheral neuropathy.**"  
  * **Impact:** "Peripheral neuropathy" due to diabetes is a common **CC**. This single phrase changes the DRG from 473 to **MS-DRG 472** and increases payment by **$6,000**.  
  * **Further Improvement:** The physician notes:  
  * "**Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus with diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) on admission.**"  
  * **Impact:** "Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)" is a **MCC**. This diagnosis would classify the patient into **MS-DRG 471**, increasing the payment by an additional **$9,000**.

#### **Case 2: The Heart Failure Patient 🫀**

* **Before CDI:** The physician's note lists "Congestive Heart Failure."  
* **Improved Documentation:** The physician notes:  
  * "**Chronic systolic heart failure, stable on admission.**"  
  * **Impact:** "Chronic systolic heart failure" is a **CC**. This moves the case from 473 to **MS-DRG 472**, increasing payment by **$6,000**.  
  * **Further Improvement:** The physician notes:  
  * "**Acute systolic heart failure exacerbation, requiring diuresis and inpatient management.**"  
  * **Impact:** "Acute systolic heart failure exacerbation" is a **MCC**. This would be documented as the patient's principal diagnosis, moving the case to **MS-DRG 471**, for an additional **$9,000** in payment.

#### **Case 3: The CKD Patient 💧**

* **Before CDI:** The physician's note lists "Chronic Kidney Disease."  
* **Improved Documentation:** The physician notes:  
  * "**Chronic kidney disease, Stage 3\.**"

  * **Further Improvement:** The physician notes:  
  * "**Chronic kidney disease, Stage 5, with acute-on-chronic kidney failure, requiring hemodialysis.**"  
  * **Impact:** "Chronic kidney failure, Stage 5" and "Acute-on-chronic kidney failure" are **MCCs**. This documentation would classify the case into **MS-DRG 471**, increasing payment by an additional **$9,000**.

### **Other Common CCs & MCCs**

Here are other examples of common conditions that can be documented with greater specificity to impact the DRG assignment:

* **Malnutrition:** The CDI specialist identifies clinical signs of malnutrition (e.g., low albumin, BMI, weight loss). The physician documents: "**Severe protein-calorie malnutrition.**" This is a **MCC**.  
* **Anemia:** The physician documents: "**Anemia due to chronic disease**" (a CC) vs. "**Acute blood loss anemia**" (a **MCC**).  
* **Hypertension:** Simple hypertension is not a CC. But documenting "**Hypertensive heart disease with heart failure**" makes it a **CC**, or if it becomes a major comorbidity, a **MCC**.

By providing a clear, precise, and specific diagnosis, physicians accurately reflect the patient's severity of illness and the resources consumed, which is crucial for appropriate hospital reimbursement and for demonstrating the hospital's true case mix index and quality outcomes.

Here are common types of conditions that frequently serve as CCs or MCCs for spinal fusion procedures.

### **Common MCCs (Major Complications/Comorbidities) 🩺**

MCCs are conditions that significantly increase resource consumption and are generally more severe than CCs. For a cervical spinal fusion, some examples of conditions that often qualify as MCCs include:

* **Acute respiratory failure**: This can be a complication of anesthesia or surgery.  
* **Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) with pulmonary embolism (PE)**: A severe postoperative complication.  
* **Systemic infections**: Sepsis or septic shock.  
* **Chronic respiratory conditions**: For example, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) with acute exacerbation.  
* **Major cardiovascular events**: Myocardial infarction or stroke.

### **Common CCs (Complications/Comorbidities) ⚕️**

CCs are conditions that also increase resource consumption but are less severe than MCCs. They are often chronic conditions that affect the patient's overall health and recovery. Examples for a cervical spinal fusion may include:

* **Diabetes mellitus**: Poorly controlled diabetes can impact healing and increase infection risk.  
* **Chronic kidney disease (CKD)**: Renal function can affect drug metabolism and overall recovery.  
* **Hypertension**: Uncontrolled blood pressure can complicate surgery and anesthesia.  
* **Obesity**: A body mass index (BMI) of 40 or greater is often a CC. Obesity can complicate surgical procedures and increase the risk of postoperative complications.  
* **Anemia**: Severe anemia often requires a transfusion and can be a marker of other underlying issues.  
* **Electrolyte imbalances**: Conditions like hyponatremia or hyperkalemia that require management.
