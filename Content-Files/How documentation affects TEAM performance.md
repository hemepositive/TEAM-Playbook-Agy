A successful Clinical Documentation Integrity (CDI) program is the strategic core for managing value-based payment models like the CMS Transforming Episode Accountability Model (TEAM). CDI experts are clear that meticulous documentation of DRG-altering comorbidities and HCCs is not just a best practice—it is the primary way to ensure fair reimbursement and improve quality metrics.

### **Why Documentation of DRG-Altering Comorbidities and HCCs is Crucial**

The CMS TEAM model operates on a risk-adjusted methodology, meaning the target price for each surgical episode is tailored to the expected complexity of the patient. Incomplete or inaccurate documentation leads to an artificially low target price, which sets your hospital up for financial loss from the start. CDI experts emphasize that you must "document to the highest level of specificity" to capture a complete clinical picture of the patient.

* **DRG-Altering Comorbidities (CCs/MCCs):** These are conditions that directly affect the assigned Medicare Severity-Diagnosis Related Group (MS-DRG). The presence of a Major Complication/Comorbidity (MCC) or a Complication/Comorbidity (CC) demonstrates a patient's increased severity of illness and higher resource utilization. Proper documentation of these conditions is essential for assigning a higher-weighted MS-DRG, which leads to a higher, more appropriate target price for the entire episode of care.  
* **Hierarchical Condition Categories (HCCs):** HCCs are a risk adjustment model used by CMS to estimate a patient's future healthcare costs based on their chronic conditions. While primarily associated with Medicare Advantage plans, similar risk adjustment methodologies are used in models like TEAM. Documenting active, chronic conditions annually confirms a patient's baseline clinical complexity. This documentation contributes to a higher risk score for the patient, which in turn raises the risk-adjusted target price for their episode.

### **Examples of Impactful Documentation**

Here are specific examples of how documenting certain conditions impacts TEAM's financial and quality metrics.

#### **Example 1: Spinal Fusion Episode**

* **Incomplete Documentation:** A physician's note for a patient undergoing spinal fusion simply states "Diabetes."  
* **Impact on TEAM:** This documentation is insufficient for risk adjustment. It does not reflect the patient's true complexity. The hospital's target price is based on a patient with uncomplicated diabetes, which may be significantly lower than the actual cost of care due to diabetic complications.  
* **Impactful Documentation:** The physician's note states: "Type 2 diabetes mellitus with diabetic neuropathy and chronic kidney disease, stage 3."  
* **Positive Outcome:** This specific documentation captures a significant comorbidity (chronic kidney disease) and an HCC (diabetic neuropathy). It directly impacts the MS-DRG to a more complex and higher-weighted tier, leading to a higher target price. This also improves the hospital's quality metrics by accurately reflecting the patient population's severity of illness, making readmission and complication rates more fairly comparable to other hospitals.

#### **Example 2: Surgical Hip and Femur Fracture Treatment (SHFFT) Episode**

* **Incomplete Documentation:** A patient is admitted with a hip fracture and the physician notes "Malnutrition."  
* **Impact on TEAM:** "Malnutrition" is vague. The hospital may not be able to code for a specific, severe form of malnutrition.  
* **Impactful Documentation:** The physician's note includes: "Protein-calorie malnutrition, severe, related to emaciated appearance with BMI 16.5 and non-healing wounds."  
* **Positive Outcome:** This detailed note captures a **Major Complication/Comorbidity (MCC)**. The addition of an MCC will increase the MS-DRG to a higher level, leading to a significantly higher target price. It also tells a story about the patient's complexity, which is essential for risk-adjusting quality metrics like complications and length of stay.

#### **Example 3: Major Bowel Procedure Episode**

* **Incomplete Documentation:** A patient undergoes a major bowel procedure, and the physician notes: "History of heart failure."  
* **Impact on TEAM:** "History of" suggests the condition is no longer active. The coder cannot use this to assign a CC or an HCC unless the physician specifies that the condition is impacting the current hospital stay.  
* **Impactful Documentation:** The physician's note clarifies: "History of chronic systolic congestive heart failure, which is currently being monitored and managed with IV Lasix."  
* **Positive Outcome:** By documenting that the condition is "monitored and managed," the physician establishes that it is a current, active condition impacting the hospital stay. This can be coded as a CC, leading to a higher MS-DRG and a more appropriate target price. This also helps in accurately risk-adjusting the hospital's performance for readmission rates and other quality measures, as a patient with active heart failure is at a higher risk for complications.

### **How this Documentation Impacts Quality Metrics**

Accurate documentation of comorbidities and HCCs is not just about financial gain; it is critical for a fair and defensible quality profile. CMS uses risk adjustment to ensure that hospitals treating sicker, more complex patient populations are not unfairly penalized on quality metrics.

For example, a hospital with a high number of patients with documented heart failure, diabetes with complications, and malnutrition will have a higher expected readmission rate. If these conditions are documented, CMS's risk adjustment models will factor them in, leading to a more favorable quality score and a higher payment adjustment. Conversely, if these conditions are not documented, the hospital's readmission rate may appear to be higher than expected, potentially leading to a financial penalty.

The key is to invest in robust CDI programs and IT tools that help physicians document with this level of specificity. This ensures a complete and accurate portrayal of the patient's condition, which is the only way to succeed financially and clinically in the TEAM model.

As Chief Medical Officer and Medical Director of IT, you'll be well aware that an effective Clinical Documentation Integrity (CDI) program is the bedrock of success for any value-based payment model. The CMS Transforming Episode Accountability Model (TEAM) is no exception. CDI experts are emphasizing that a robust documentation strategy is crucial for two primary reasons related to DRG-altering comorbidities and Hierarchical Condition Categories (HCCs): **accurate risk adjustment** and **appropriate reimbursement**.

Here's a breakdown of what CDI experts are saying and why it's so important for your hospital's success under the TEAM model.

### **1\. The Critical Role of Documentation for Risk Adjustment**

CMS's bundled payment models, including TEAM, are built on a risk-adjusted methodology.1 This means that CMS sets a target price for each episode of care that is adjusted based on the patient's expected clinical complexity. The more complex the patient, the higher the expected cost of care, and therefore the higher the target price.

* **DRG-Altering Comorbidities:** CDI experts are stressing that physicians must meticulously document all significant complications and comorbidities (CCs and MCCs) that impact a patient's care. These conditions directly influence the assigned MS-DRG (Medicare Severity-Diagnosis Related Group).2 For example, a patient with a hip fracture may have an MS-DRG with or without an MCC.3 A well-documented MCC, such as acute kidney injury or sepsis, will move the patient to a higher-paying, more complex MS-DRG, which translates to a higher target price for the entire episode.4 Without this documentation, the hospital is held accountable to a target price for a less complex patient, a situation that is almost certain to result in a financial loss.  
* **Hierarchical Condition Categories (HCCs):** The CMS TEAM model, similar to other value-based models, uses a risk adjustment factor that considers a patient's historical health status. This is often based on HCCs from claims data spanning three years prior to the hospitalization. CDI specialists are advising that documentation in the inpatient setting must "capture" these chronic conditions. While the HCCs may already exist in the patient's claims history, the physician's documentation confirms that these conditions are still active and relevant to the patient's overall health and the complexity of their care. A patient with a major bowel procedure and a documented history of congestive heart failure and diabetes with complications will have a higher-risk profile, and CMS's pricing model will reflect that, increasing the target price.

### **2\. Ensuring Appropriate Reimbursement and Avoiding Financial Loss**

The financial success of your hospital in the TEAM model hinges on your ability to deliver care below the risk-adjusted target price. The target price itself is the starting point, and it's essential that it accurately reflects the patient's clinical picture.

* **Accuracy from the Outset:** CDI professionals are emphasizing that the CDI and coding teams must perform concurrent reviews of the inpatient record. This allows them to query physicians in real time to clarify and add specificity to documentation. The goal is to ensure the final MS-DRG accurately reflects the patient's severity of illness and risk, providing a fair and appropriate target price for the episode.  
* **Specific Documentation for Complex Conditions:** Physicians are being educated to document not just the diagnosis but also the specificity of the condition, its acuity, and its impact on the patient's management and hospital course.5 For example, instead of just "diabetes," documentation should specify "uncontrolled type 2 diabetes with chronic kidney disease" if applicable. This level of detail is crucial for capturing the correct CCs, MCCs, and HCCs that will increase the risk-adjusted target price.  
* **Impact on the C-Suite:** From your perspective as a C-suite leader, this is not just a coding or billing issue; it's a strategic priority. Incorrect or incomplete documentation leads to an artificially low target price, which sets your hospital up for failure from the beginning. Your IT and CDI teams must work in lockstep to implement systems and workflows that support this level of documentation. This includes providing physicians with actionable data and training on the "why" behind the need for detailed documentation, connecting it directly to your hospital's financial and quality goals.

In summary, CDI experts are making a clear case: in the CMS TEAM model, your hospital's financial performance and ability to meet quality goals are inextricably linked to the quality of your clinical documentation. It's no longer enough to document for just billing; you must document for risk adjustment to ensure your hospital is fairly compensated for the complexity of the patients it serves.

# The impact of poor documentation for the upcoming TEAMS

Here are the key concerns:

\#\# Financial Implications

\*\*Revenue Loss\*\*: Poor documentation can lead to delayed or denied reimbursements\[3\]. With TEAM's bundled payment structure, inadequate documentation of the necessity and details of services provided during the 30-day episode of care could result in substantial revenue losses\[1\]\[3\].

\*\*Audit Risk\*\*: Inaccurate documentation increases the likelihood of audits, which could lead to penalties and further financial instability\[3\]. Under TEAM, this risk is heightened as the model covers all items and services under Medicare Parts A and B during the episode\[1\].

\*\*Cash Flow Issues\*\*: Consistent documentation errors can create bottlenecks in the billing process, disrupting cash flow and making it difficult to cover operational expenses\[3\]\[5\].

\#\# Quality and Performance Metrics

\*\*Quality Score Impact\*\*: TEAM incorporates a quality score methodology that affects payment\[1\]. Poor documentation could lead to lower quality scores, directly impacting the hospital's reimbursement under this model.

\*\*Missed Opportunities\*\*: Inadequate documentation may fail to capture the full complexity of patient cases, potentially leading to lower risk-adjusted payments and missed opportunities for shared savings under TEAM\[1\].

\#\# Operational Challenges

\*\*Difficulty in Care Coordination\*\*: TEAM aims to facilitate increased cooperation between various care providers\[1\]. Poor documentation can hinder this coordination, potentially leading to inefficiencies and higher costs.

\*\*Inaccurate Performance Analysis\*\*: Without proper documentation, it becomes challenging to accurately analyze the hospital's performance under TEAM, making it difficult to identify areas for improvement and cost reduction\[2\].

\#\# Patient Care and Safety

\*\*Compromised Care Quality\*\*: Vague or incomplete medical records can lead to misinformed treatment decisions, potentially resulting in poor patient outcomes and increased readmissions\[3\]. This could negatively affect the hospital's performance under TEAM.

\#\# Strategic Implications

\*\*Competitive Disadvantage\*\*: As TEAM is designed to incentivize better care coordination and cost savings, hospitals with poor documentation practices may find themselves at a significant competitive disadvantage\[1\]\[2\].

\*\*Limited Growth Potential\*\*: Chronic documentation issues can stifle the hospital's growth potential by limiting its ability to maximize reimbursements and invest in improvements\[3\]\[5\].

In conclusion, as we prepare for the implementation of TEAM in 2026, improving our clinical documentation practices must be a top priority to ensure financial stability, maintain high-quality patient care, and position our hospital for success under this new bundled payment model.
