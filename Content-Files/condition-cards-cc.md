# Hyponatremia

**Classification:** CC
**Applies to:** All five TEAM procedures

[PEARL] A sodium of 128 is a lab value. Hyponatremia is a diagnosis. If you ordered a sodium restriction, fluid restriction, or hypertonic saline — you diagnosed and treated hyponatremia. Write it.

## Document This

✅ "Hyponatremia, sodium 124, likely SIADH in the setting of pneumonia. Free water restriction initiated."

✅ "Severe hyponatremia, Na 118, with neurological symptoms. Hypertonic saline infusion started, nephrology consulted."

✅ "Hyponatremia, hypervolemic, in the setting of decompensated heart failure. Diuresis initiated."

## Not This

❌ "Na 128 — continue monitoring." — Lab value. Not a diagnosis.

❌ "Low sodium, likely dilutional." — Clinical interpretation without a diagnosis.

❌ "Electrolyte abnormality." — Unspecified. Not codeable as hyponatremia.

## Rules

- Specify the type when known: hypovolemic, euvolemic (SIADH), or hypervolemic.
- Severe hyponatremia with neurological symptoms (seizure, coma, obtundation) carries higher severity weight — document symptoms when present.
- Hyponatremia is distinct from hypernatremia — both are CCs and should be documented separately when both occur.

# Hypernatremia

**Classification:** CC
**Applies to:** SHFFT · Major Bowel · Any patient with impaired free water access

[PEARL] Elderly, cognitively impaired, and postoperative patients are at high risk for hypernatremia. If sodium is consistently above 145 and you are managing it — document the diagnosis.

## Document This

✅ "Hypernatremia, sodium 152, likely due to insensible losses and reduced free water intake. Hypotonic IV fluids started."

✅ "Hypernatremia in the setting of central diabetes insipidus. Desmopressin initiated."

## Not This

❌ "Sodium elevated — increase free water." — Order placed, diagnosis not documented.

❌ "Dehydration." — Dehydration is a symptom. Hypernatremia is the specific metabolic diagnosis.

## Rules

- Link to etiology when known: insufficient intake, excessive losses (GI, renal, insensible), diabetes insipidus.
- Document treatment: free water replacement, hypotonic fluids, correction rate.

# Metabolic Acidosis / Alkalosis

**Classification:** CC
**Applies to:** Major Bowel · CABG · SHFFT

[PEARL] Arterial blood gases are ordered because you suspected an acid-base problem. If the results confirmed it and you treated it — document the diagnosis.

## Document This

✅ "Metabolic acidosis, anion gap 22, likely lactic acidosis in the setting of sepsis. Bicarbonate trending."

✅ "Metabolic alkalosis secondary to prolonged NG suction and volume contraction. Chloride replacement initiated."

✅ "Mixed metabolic acidosis and respiratory alkalosis in the setting of hepatic failure."

## Not This

❌ "ABG showing pH 7.28, bicarb 14 — continue monitoring." — Abnormal values documented, diagnosis not written.

❌ "Lactic acid elevated." — Lab finding. Lactic acidosis is the diagnosis.

## Rules

- Specify type: metabolic vs. respiratory, acidosis vs. alkalosis.
- Link to etiology: lactic acidosis from sepsis or ischemia, hyperchloremic acidosis from saline, contraction alkalosis from diuresis.
- Mixed disorders — when both metabolic and respiratory components are present — should be documented as such.

# Pleural Effusion

**Classification:** CC
**Applies to:** CABG · Major Bowel · Any patient with prolonged hospitalization

[PEARL] Pleural effusion develops predictably after CABG and in many critically ill patients. It is imaged, it is often drained, and it is almost never documented as a diagnosis. If you ordered a thoracentesis or saw it on imaging and it changed management — write it.

## Document This

✅ "Bilateral pleural effusions, moderate, post-CABG. Thoracentesis performed on the right, 800mL exudative fluid removed."

✅ "Pleural effusion, left-sided, contributing to respiratory compromise. Thoracentesis planned."

✅ "Small bilateral pleural effusions, clinically insignificant, noted on CXR. No intervention at this time." — Even when not treated, document it if it's clinically noted.

## Not This

❌ "CXR with blunting of costophrenic angles." — Radiographic description. Not a diagnosis.

❌ "Fluid overload with pulmonary changes." — Does not capture pleural effusion specifically.

## Rules

- Specify laterality: left, right, bilateral.
- Specify size when known: small, moderate, large.
- Specify if exudative vs. transudative when thoracentesis has been performed.
- Link to etiology when known: post-CABG, heart failure, malignancy, pneumonia.

# Ileus

**Classification:** CC
**Applies to:** Major Bowel · Any abdominal surgery

[PEARL] NG tube placement, NPO status, and prolonged absence of bowel function are treatments. Ileus is the diagnosis. If you placed an NG tube for decompression — you diagnosed and treated ileus. Write it.

## Document This

✅ "Postoperative ileus — absent bowel sounds, abdominal distension, NG tube output 1,200mL/day. NPO, decompression ongoing."

✅ "Prolonged ileus following sigmoid colectomy, day 5. KUB showing dilated loops without obstruction. Conservative management continued."

## Not This

❌ "Slow GI recovery — NPO, NG tube in place." — Clinical situation documented, diagnosis not.

❌ "Not tolerating diet." — Symptom. Not a diagnosis.

❌ "Abdominal distension." — Finding. Not a diagnosis.

## Rules

- Distinguish ileus from small bowel obstruction — KUB or CT findings help; document which is present.
- Postoperative ileus is expected after bowel surgery but still warrants documentation when it prolongs the stay or requires intervention.
- Prolonged ileus (beyond the expected 2–3 days) is more clinically significant and should be clearly named.

# Peritonitis

**Classification:** CC (when secondary to the procedure or as comorbidity) · MCC (when it is the primary driver of sepsis)
**Applies to:** Major Bowel

[PEARL] When a bowel perforation causes peritonitis and sepsis, the peritonitis is part of what makes the case severe. Document it explicitly — not just the perforation.

## Document This

✅ "Feculent peritonitis secondary to sigmoid perforation. Source control achieved with Hartmann's procedure. Broad-spectrum antibiotics continued."

✅ "Loculated peritonitis, right lower quadrant, from perforated appendix. Drainage and washout performed."

## Not This

❌ "Perforated sigmoid — taken to OR." — Perforation documented, peritonitis not.

❌ "Abdominal contamination intraoperatively." — Operative finding, not a diagnosis.

## Rules

- Link peritonitis to source: perforated diverticulitis, anastomotic leak, perforated peptic ulcer.
- Specify type when known: feculent, bilious, purulent, loculated.
- When peritonitis is the source of sepsis, document sepsis separately with peritonitis as the source.

# C. difficile Infection

**Classification:** CC
**Applies to:** Major Bowel · Any prolonged antibiotic course

[PEARL] A positive C. diff toxin is a lab result. C. difficile colitis is a diagnosis. If you started fidaxomicin or oral vancomycin — you treated a C. diff infection. Document it.

## Document This

✅ "C. difficile colitis, confirmed on stool toxin PCR. Oral vancomycin started. Contact precautions in place."

✅ "Recurrent C. difficile infection — second episode within 3 months. Fidaxomicin initiated."

✅ "Severe C. difficile colitis with ileus and leukocytosis 28k. GI and surgery consulted."

## Not This

❌ "C. diff positive — starting vanco." — Treatment started, diagnosis not documented.

❌ "Diarrhea, C. diff being ruled out." — Pending diagnosis. Document once confirmed.

## Rules

- Specify severity when applicable: non-severe, severe (WBC >15k or creatinine >1.5× baseline), or fulminant (hypotension, ileus, toxic megacolon).
- Recurrent episodes should be documented as recurrent — affects treatment decisions and coding.
- Contact precautions should be documented — implies confirmed infection.

# DVT (Acute)

**Classification:** CC
**Applies to:** LEJR · SHFFT · Spinal Fusion · Any procedure with VTE risk

[PEARL] DVT prophylaxis is ordered for patients at risk. DVT treatment is ordered when it occurs. If you switched from prophylactic to therapeutic anticoagulation — that transition implies a DVT diagnosis. Document it.

## Document This

✅ "Acute deep vein thrombosis, right popliteal vein, confirmed on duplex ultrasound. Therapeutic anticoagulation initiated."

✅ "Acute proximal DVT, left femoral-popliteal. Hematology consulted regarding anticoagulation in the setting of recent surgery."

## Not This

❌ "DVT prophylaxis — Lovenox." — Prophylaxis is not treatment of a DVT.

❌ "Concern for DVT — duplex ordered." — Suspected diagnosis. Document once confirmed.

❌ "Leg swelling, ultrasound negative." — If negative, correctly not documented as DVT.

## Rules

- Specify location: proximal (femoral, iliac, popliteal) vs. distal (tibial, peroneal). Proximal carries higher clinical significance.
- Specify laterality.
- Distinguish acute from chronic — chronic DVT codes differently and carries less severity weight.
- PE and DVT occurring together should both be documented.

# Atelectasis

**Classification:** CC
**Applies to:** CABG · Major Bowel · Spinal Fusion — any procedure with prolonged anesthesia

[PEARL] Atelectasis is the most common pulmonary complication after major surgery and one of the most consistently underdocumented. If you ordered incentive spirometry, chest PT, or bronchoscopy for mucus plugging — the clinical picture likely supports atelectasis.

## Document This

✅ "Atelectasis, right lower lobe, post-CABG. Chest physiotherapy and incentive spirometry initiated. Respiratory therapy following."

✅ "Significant atelectasis with lobar collapse, left lower lobe. Bronchoscopy performed for mucus plug clearance."

## Not This

❌ "CXR with basilar haziness." — Radiographic finding. Not a diagnosis.

❌ "Incentive spirometry ordered." — Treatment. Not a diagnosis.

## Rules

- Specify location when known: right lower lobe, bilateral basilar, left lower lobe.
- Significant atelectasis with lobar collapse or requiring bronchoscopy is more clinically meaningful and should be documented with that detail.
- Distinguish atelectasis from pneumonia when both are possible — the distinction affects code assignment and clinical clarity.

# Hypertensive Crisis / Accelerated Hypertension

**Classification:** CC
**Applies to:** CABG · LEJR · Any procedure in a hypertensive patient

[PEARL] Routine postoperative hypertension is expected and not a diagnosis. Hypertensive urgency (severe elevation without end-organ damage) and hypertensive emergency (severe elevation with end-organ damage) are diagnoses. If you gave IV antihypertensives or called cardiology — document why.

## Document This

✅ "Hypertensive urgency — BP 198/112 persistent despite oral medications. IV labetalol given, blood pressure controlled."

✅ "Hypertensive emergency with acute hypertensive encephalopathy. Nicardipine drip initiated, neurology consulted."

✅ "Accelerated hypertension, BP consistently above 180/100 perioperatively. Antihypertensive regimen adjusted."

## Not This

❌ "Hypertension — BP elevated, continue home meds." — Stable chronic hypertension. Not a hypertensive crisis.

❌ "BP 195/110 — gave hydralazine." — Treatment documented, diagnosis not.

## Rules

- Routine hypertension in a known hypertensive patient is not a crisis.
- Hypertensive urgency = BP typically >180/120 without end-organ damage.
- Hypertensive emergency = severe BP with end-organ damage: encephalopathy, acute MI, dissection, acute HF, AKI.
- Document the specific end-organ effect when present — it affects both code assignment and clinical management.

# Cellulitis

**Classification:** CC
**Applies to:** LEJR · SHFFT · Any surgical patient with wound complication

[PEARL] Wound redness and warmth are findings. Cellulitis is a diagnosis. If you started antibiotics for a wound infection — document the diagnosis.

## Document This

✅ "Cellulitis, right lower extremity, extending 4cm beyond wound margins. Oral cephalexin started."

✅ "Postoperative wound cellulitis, left hip incision site, erythema and induration. IV antibiotics initiated given failure of oral therapy."

## Not This

❌ "Wound redness — antibiotics started." — Finding plus treatment. Not a diagnosis.

❌ "Surgical site infection concern." — Concern is not a diagnosis. Document once you have made the clinical determination.

## Rules

- Specify site and laterality.
- Distinguish cellulitis from surgical site infection (SSI) — SSI has specific CDC criteria and HAC implications for certain procedures.
- Severity matters: cellulitis requiring IV antibiotics vs. oral antibiotics is clinically and documentarily different.
- For orthopedic patients, document whether infection is superficial (above fascia) or deep (below fascia, involving joint) — the distinction affects coding and clinical management significantly.

# Osteomyelitis

**Classification:** CC
**Applies to:** LEJR · Spinal Fusion · Any procedure with bone involvement

[PEARL] Osteomyelitis is a specific diagnosis that requires explicit physician documentation. "Bone infection" or "infected hardware" is not codeable without naming osteomyelitis or a specific infectious diagnosis.

## Document This

✅ "Acute osteomyelitis, left proximal femur, confirmed on MRI and bone biopsy — Staphylococcus aureus. IV vancomycin initiated, orthopedics and infectious disease co-managing."

✅ "Chronic osteomyelitis, lumbar spine (L4), with sinus tract formation. Surgical debridement planned."

## Not This

❌ "Bone infection — cultures pending." — Document once confirmed.

❌ "Infected hardware." — Describes the clinical situation, not the diagnosis. Osteomyelitis and periprosthetic joint infection are distinct diagnoses.

## Rules

- Specify acute vs. chronic.
- Specify site and organism when known.
- Periprosthetic joint infection (PJI) is a distinct diagnosis from osteomyelitis — use the appropriate term for the clinical picture.
- Vertebral osteomyelitis in the spinal fusion population should be documented with level, organism, and whether hardware is involved.

# Cardiomyopathy (Non-Ischemic)

**Classification:** CC
**Applies to:** CABG · LEJR · Any patient with reduced EF

[PEARL] Reduced ejection fraction without a named underlying cause codes as heart failure — not cardiomyopathy. If the patient has dilated cardiomyopathy, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, or another specified type, name it alongside the heart failure diagnosis. Both conditions code separately.

## Document This

✅ "Dilated cardiomyopathy, non-ischemic, EF 25%. Managed with guideline-directed medical therapy."

✅ "Hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy — gradient 45mmHg on echo, on metoprolol."

✅ "Peripartum cardiomyopathy, EF recovering from nadir of 20%. Current EF 38%."

## Not This

❌ "Poor EF — continue cardiac meds." — EF noted, cardiomyopathy diagnosis not written.

❌ "Cardiomegaly on CXR." — Radiographic finding. Not a diagnosis.

## Rules

- Specify type: dilated, hypertrophic (obstructive vs. non-obstructive), restrictive, arrhythmogenic, peripartum, stress (Takotsubo).
- Ischemic cardiomyopathy (caused by CAD) codes from the ischemic heart disease category — it is distinct from non-ischemic cardiomyopathy.
- Document EF when available — supports clinical validity.
- Both cardiomyopathy and heart failure should be coded when both are present.

# Coagulopathy (Non-DIC)

**Classification:** CC
**Applies to:** CABG · Major Bowel · SHFFT — patients on anticoagulants or with liver disease

[PEARL] Coagulopathy in the context of liver disease, massive transfusion, or anticoagulant therapy is a documentable diagnosis. "PT/PTT elevated" is a lab value. If the coagulopathy changed your management — it needs a name.

## Document This

✅ "Coagulopathy secondary to hepatic dysfunction — INR 3.2, FFP administered preoperatively."

✅ "Acquired coagulopathy in the setting of massive transfusion. Ratio-based resuscitation protocol initiated."

✅ "Coagulopathy due to warfarin — supratherapeutic INR 6.1. Vitamin K administered, held warfarin."

## Not This

❌ "INR elevated — FFP given." — Lab value and treatment. Not a diagnosis.

❌ "Patient on blood thinners." — Medication documentation, not a coagulopathy diagnosis.

## Rules

- Link to etiology: liver disease, medications, dilutional, massive transfusion, vitamin K deficiency.
- Distinguish from DIC — DIC has its own higher-severity code and criteria.
- Document the INR or PT/PTT level in context with the diagnosis for audit support.

# Malnutrition (Moderate)

**Classification:** CC
**Applies to:** All five TEAM procedures

[PEARL] If the patient isn't severely malnourished — they may still be moderately malnourished. Moderate malnutrition is a CC. When you can't reach severe, reach for moderate. Both require physician documentation. Neither requires the dietitian to do it for you.

## Document This

✅ "Moderate protein-calorie malnutrition — 7% weight loss over 3 months, mild muscle wasting on exam, reduced intake in setting of chronic illness. Consistent with ASPEN criteria for moderate malnutrition. Oral nutritional supplements initiated."

✅ "Moderate malnutrition in the context of active malignancy — reduced intake, early satiety, mild-moderate muscle loss. Dietitian-guided nutrition plan in place."

## Not This

❌ "Nutritional deficiency — dietitian following." — Not a physician diagnosis.

❌ "Patient eating poorly." — Observation. Not a diagnosis.

## Rules

- Moderate malnutrition = CC. Severe malnutrition = MCC. The distinction requires physician documentation of severity.
- ASPEN criteria: at least 2 of the following in the context of illness — energy deficit, weight loss (moderate: 5% in 1 month or 10% in 6 months), muscle wasting, fat wasting, fluid accumulation, reduced grip strength.
- When the clinical picture is borderline between moderate and severe, document your clinical reasoning for the severity determination.
- Even mild malnutrition (unspecified) captures a CC — better than no nutrition diagnosis at all.

# Acute Pancreatitis

**Classification:** MCC (acute) · CC (chronic)
**Applies to:** Major Bowel

[PEARL] Acute pancreatitis severity should be documented. Mild pancreatitis is not an MCC. Severe acute pancreatitis with organ failure is. The distinction is clinically important and documentarily impactful.

## Document This

✅ "Acute pancreatitis, mild, likely gallstone etiology. NPO, IV fluids, pain management. Lipase 1,840."

✅ "Severe acute pancreatitis with acute kidney injury and respiratory failure — organ failure present. ICU admission, nutrition via NJ tube."

✅ "Necrotizing pancreatitis — CT showing >30% pancreatic necrosis. Interventional radiology and surgery consulted."

## Not This

❌ "Elevated lipase — pancreatitis?" — Query framing. Not a diagnosis.

❌ "Abdominal pain, lipase elevated." — Symptom and lab. Not a diagnosis.

## Rules

- Specify severity: mild (no organ failure), moderately severe (transient organ failure), severe (persistent organ failure >48h).
- Specify etiology when known: gallstone, alcohol, hypertriglyceridemia, medication-induced, idiopathic.
- Necrotizing pancreatitis and infected pancreatic necrosis are distinct, higher-severity diagnoses that should be named explicitly when present.
- Chronic pancreatitis = CC; acute pancreatitis = MCC.

# Hemiplegia / Hemiparesis

**Classification:** CC
**Applies to:** SHFFT · LEJR — patients with prior stroke

[PEARL] Patients with prior stroke often have residual motor deficits that directly affect surgical risk, rehabilitation potential, and post-acute planning. Documenting "history of stroke" captures the prior event but not the active functional deficit. The deficit is what codes as a CC.

## Document This

✅ "Left hemiparesis — residual motor deficit from right MCA stroke 2 years ago. Baseline ambulation with hemi-walker. Present on admission."

✅ "Right hemiplegia following prior hemorrhagic stroke. Fully dependent for transfers. Affects surgical planning and rehab disposition."

## Not This

❌ "History of stroke, left-sided weakness." — History of codes as resolved event. Weakness is a symptom. Neither captures the CC.

❌ "CVA in past." — Prior event only. No functional deficit documented.

## Rules

- Document the active functional deficit — hemiplegia (complete) or hemiparesis (partial) — not just the prior event.
- Specify side: left vs. right.
- Establish that the deficit is present on admission — relevant for POA status.
- Document functional baseline: ambulation status, transfer status, grip strength — supports the clinical picture.

# Schizophrenia

**Classification:** CC
**Applies to:** Any procedure in a patient with serious mental illness

[PEARL] Schizophrenia is one of the most consistent predictors of complicated hospital courses, longer length of stay, and higher readmission risk. It is also consistently underdocumented in surgical patients. If it is active and being managed — document it.

## Document This

✅ "Schizophrenia, paranoid type, stable on risperidone. Continued home antipsychotic during hospitalization. Psychiatry consulted for behavioral management."

✅ "Schizoaffective disorder — home quetiapine continued. Behavioral health team involved in discharge planning."

## Not This

❌ "Psychiatric history." — Not a diagnosis. Not codeable.

❌ "On antipsychotics." — Medication without diagnosis.

❌ "Schizophrenia — history." — History framing implies resolved. Schizophrenia is a chronic active condition.

## Rules

- Specify type when known: paranoid, disorganized, catatonic, undifferentiated, schizoaffective.
- Document that it is active and currently managed.
- Continuing home antipsychotics during hospitalization implies active diagnosis — write it.
- Unspecified schizophrenia still codes as a CC — better than no diagnosis.
