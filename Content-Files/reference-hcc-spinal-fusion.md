# HCC Reference: Spinal Fusion

Spinal fusion has 13 beneficiary-level HCC risk adjustment variables. The spinal fusion population is clinically heterogeneous — it spans young adults with degenerative disc disease, middle-aged patients with spondylolisthesis or deformity, and elderly patients with stenosis and significant medical comorbidities. That heterogeneity means the HCC profile of any given patient is harder to predict than in the joint replacement population, and documentation gaps are more variable.

Because spinal fusion DRGs span 13 families with variable tier structures, the risk-adjusted target price is especially important: a patient with multiple documented HCCs in a complex procedure family generates a substantially higher target price than the same patient at a base DRG with no HCC documentation.

[PEARL] The spinal fusion population skews younger than LEJR or SHFFT, but still carries significant metabolic and cardiovascular comorbidity burden. Do not assume a 55-year-old lumbar fusion patient has no HCC-relevant conditions. Obesity hypoventilation syndrome, diabetes with neuropathy, and COPD are common in the chronic pain and degenerative spine population and are all HCC-relevant.

[NOTE] HCCs must be re-documented each calendar year. For elective spinal fusion patients, the pre-operative optimization visit, the anesthesia pre-assessment, or any outpatient encounter in the 180 days before surgery is the documentation window. Post-surgical HCC documentation during the hospitalization contributes to risk adjustment for that episode but misses patients whose conditions were present but undocumented pre-operatively.

## Spinal Fusion-Specific Documentation Priorities

**Spinal cord disorders/myelopathy** (HCC 72) is unique to the spinal fusion HCC set and not present in LEJR or SHFFT. Myelopathy — spinal cord compression producing neurological deficit — is the primary indication for decompression and fusion in many cervical and thoracic cases. Documenting "cervical myelopathy" rather than "cervical stenosis" or "neck pain" captures HCC 72 and reflects the actual clinical picture. The distinction matters both for risk adjustment and for clinical accuracy: a patient operated on for myelopathy has a different expected outcome profile than one operated on for radiculopathy.

**Vertebral osteomyelitis/bone infection** (HCC 21) is present in spinal fusion but not in the joint replacement HCC sets. When spinal fusion is performed for infectious indication — discitis, vertebral osteomyelitis — this HCC should be captured. Document the organism, the level(s) involved, and the infectious etiology explicitly.

**Depression** (HCCs 155, 158) — two tiers of depression appear in the spinal fusion HCC set, reflecting the well-established prevalence of major depression in the chronic pain and degenerative spine population. This is one of the most commonly underdocumented HCCs in this population. A patient on an antidepressant for chronic pain who also has a formal MDD diagnosis should have that diagnosis documented with current severity. "Depression, on sertraline" is not a diagnostic statement. "Major depressive disorder, moderate, currently treated with sertraline 100mg daily with partial response" is.

**Substance use disorders** (HCCs 54, 55) — chronic pain patients frequently have documented histories of opioid use disorder or alcohol use disorder. When these are active and being managed — whether in treatment or in active use — they should be documented as current diagnoses, not historical ones.

**Obesity hypoventilation syndrome** — while not on the TEAM HCC list for spinal fusion, OHS is a CC in the MS-DRG system and affects DRG tier. For the morbidly obese spine patient on home CPAP, OHS should be documented explicitly if clinically supported.

## Neurological Status Documentation

For spinal fusion patients, neurological status documentation serves double duty: it is both HCC-relevant (for myelopathy, paraplegia) and clinically critical for operative planning and outcome assessment.

[PHRASING]
❌ "Patient with cervical stenosis and weakness — recommend fusion." — "Weakness" is a symptom. No myelopathy HCC captured.
✅ "Cervical spondylotic myelopathy (G99.2) at C5-C6 with bilateral upper extremity weakness and impaired fine motor function. Modified JOA score 12/18. Proceeding with anterior cervical discectomy and fusion." — Myelopathy HCC captured; neurological severity documented.

[PHRASING]
❌ "Back pain, disc disease, proceed with lumbar fusion." — No HCC-relevant diagnoses captured.
✅ "Lumbar spondylolisthesis L4-L5 with lumbar radiculopathy, bilateral lower extremity radicular pain and paresthesias. Type 2 diabetes mellitus with diabetic peripheral neuropathy (E11.40). Major depressive disorder, moderate (F33.1), on escitalopram. Morbid obesity (E66.01), BMI 43. Proceeding with L4-L5 posterior instrumented fusion." — Multiple HCCs captured; clinical picture complete.
