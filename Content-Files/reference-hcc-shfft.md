# HCC Reference: Surgical Hip/Femur Fracture Treatment

SHFFT has the same 16 beneficiary-level HCC risk adjustment variables as LEJR — and the highest post-acute cost share of any TEAM procedure (38–54% of total episode spend). For this population, HCC documentation is especially consequential: hip fracture patients are older, frailer, carry more comorbidities, and require institutional post-acute care at far higher rates. A patient with four documented HCCs generates a meaningfully higher target price than the same patient with no documented HCCs. If those comorbidities are real but undocumented, the hospital absorbs the gap.

The SHFFT population differs from LEJR in important ways. These are frequently urgent or emergent cases — there is no pre-scheduled pre-admission encounter to capture HCCs. The admission H&P is often the only opportunity. That document carries extraordinary weight in SHFFT: it sets the POA record, establishes comorbidity baseline, and is the primary HCC capture vehicle — all at once, usually written quickly.

[PEARL] For SHFFT, the admission H&P is your HCC documentation. There is no pre-op clinic visit, no scheduled anesthesia pre-assessment, no outpatient optimization encounter. What you write in the first 24 hours is the clinical record that will determine the risk-adjusted target price for this episode.

[NOTE] HCCs must be re-documented each calendar year. Many hip fracture patients are nursing home residents or frequent hospital users — their conditions may be documented in prior records but not in the current year. The physician at admission must actively document active chronic conditions, not rely on the problem list from a prior encounter.

## SHFFT-Specific Documentation Priorities

**Dementia** (HCCs 125, 126, 127) is the highest-prevalence HCC in the hip fracture population. A substantial proportion of patients admitted for hip fracture have underlying dementia — diagnosed or undiagnosed. The admission H&P should explicitly document dementia diagnosis and severity for every patient with known cognitive impairment. "Confusion" and "altered mental status" are symptoms, not diagnoses. For an elderly patient with a known dementia diagnosis from prior records, documenting "moderate Alzheimer's dementia, previously diagnosed, on [medication]" takes one sentence and captures a significant risk adjustment variable.

**Heart failure** (HCCs 224, 225, 226) is the second-highest-impact HCC cluster for SHFFT. The specificity ladder — unspecified CHF → chronic systolic HF → acute on chronic systolic HF — represents real differences in HCC weight. Hip fracture patients often present with fluid shifts peri-operatively; if there is acute HF on top of chronic HF, document it explicitly.

**COPD** (HCC 280) is prevalent and frequently vague in admission documentation. "Pulmonary history" and "lung problems" do not code. "COPD, moderately severe, on [inhaler regimen], not currently exacerbating" does.

**Hemiplegia/Hemiparesis** (HCC 253) — hip fracture patients with prior strokes often have residual motor deficits that contributed to the fall. Document active neurological residuals, not just the history of stroke.

**Prior post-acute care use** is a CMS risk factor for SHFFT captured from claims data — it does not require physician documentation. But it underscores that this is a population with high baseline post-acute utilization, making HCC risk adjustment even more important to ensure the target price reflects actual patient complexity.

## Making the Admission H&P Count

For SHFFT, the admission H&P is doing triple work: establishing POA for secondary diagnoses, creating the primary HCC capture document, and setting the clinical baseline for the rest of the hospitalization. A well-written H&P for a hip fracture patient should explicitly name:

Every active chronic condition — by its specific, current diagnosis, not as a "history of"
Current medications that imply diagnoses — metformin implies diabetes; an ACE inhibitor and furosemide imply heart failure; a LAMA inhaler implies COPD
Functional and cognitive baseline before the fracture — this is essential for disposition planning and for documenting dementia or hemiparesis that may be masked by acute pain and delirium

[PHRASING]
❌ "PMH: DM, HTN, CHF, COPD, dementia. Presents with right hip fracture after fall." — Past medical history as a list captures nothing for HCC purposes.
✅ "Active problems: Type 2 diabetes mellitus with diabetic peripheral neuropathy (E11.40), managed with metformin and gabapentin. Chronic systolic heart failure (I50.22), NYHA Class II, stable on lisinopril and furosemide. COPD, moderate (J44.1), on tiotropium, no current exacerbation. Moderate Alzheimer's dementia (G30.1, F02.80), baseline MMSE 14/30, lives in memory care facility. Presents with acute right femoral neck fracture (S72.001A) following ground-level fall." — All HCCs captured; all POA-relevant diagnoses named and dated.
