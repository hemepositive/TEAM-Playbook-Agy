# TEAM Episode Severity & Comorbidity Capture Analysis
## Regional Benchmark & Clinical Documentation Improvement (CDI) Surgeon Guide for Huntsville Hospital

This report provides a detailed clinical and statistical analysis of comorbidity capture rates for **Huntsville Hospital (CCN 010039)** compared against **East South Central (ESC) Regional** (AL, KY, MS, TN) and **National** benchmarks. 

The Transforming Episode Accountability Model (TEAM) relies on severity-adjusted payments. If clinical documentation fails to capture a patient's true acuity via Complications or Comorbidities (CCs) and Major Complications or Comorbidities (MCCs), the hospital is reimbursed at a baseline rate that does not cover the resources required to treat complex patients.

## 2. Category Deep-Dives: Analysis & Surgeon Action Items

### 🦴 1. Lower Extremity Joint Replacement (LEJR)
* **Acuity Standing:** **Excellent.** Huntsville Hospital is the highest-volume provider in the region (203 cases) and captures MCCs on **10.34%** of cases, exceeding the national average (**8.27%**).
* **Clinical Insight:** Since LEJR is a 2-tier DRG, CCs (like baseline stage 3 CKD or uncomplicated diabetes) do not affect reimbursement. The focus must remain strictly on capturing **MCC-level** diagnoses.

> [!TIP]
> **Surgeon Action Items for LEJR:**
> 1. **Define Respiratory Distress Clearly:** Do not just write "post-op hypoxia on nasal cannula." If the patient requires high-flow oxygen, BiPAP, or aggressive respiratory therapy for acute hypoxia, document **Acute Respiratory Failure with Hypoxia (J96.01)** (MCC).
> 2. **Specify Malnutrition Severity:** Frail joint replacement patients with severe muscle wasting or major weight loss must be documented as having **Severe Protein-Calorie Malnutrition (E43)** (MCC). "Malnutrition" alone is only a CC.
> 3. **Address Delirium Promptly:** Post-operative delirium due to anesthesia, medication, or fluid shifts should be documented as **Metabolic or Toxic Encephalopathy (G93.41)** (MCC) rather than "confusion" or "altered mental status."

---

### 🦿 2. Surgical Hip & Femur Fracture Treatment (SHFFT)
* **Acuity Standing:** **Strong.** Only **6.59%** of Huntsville's cases are billed in the lowest severity tier, beating the national average of **7.49%**. Huntsville's CC capture rate (**73.08%**) is excellent.
* **Clinical Insight:** Fracture patients are typically elderly admissions following a fall. They have high rates of pre-existing dementia, osteoporosis, and electrolyte imbalances.

> [!NOTE]
> **Surgeon Action Items for SHFFT:**
> 1. **Capture Pre-existing Cognitive Decline:** Elderly fracture patients often have active dementia. Document **Dementia in other diseases** (F02.80) or **Alzheimer's Disease** (G30.9) (MCCs) if it impacts their care plan (e.g., requires constant redirection or post-op delirium precautions).
> 2. **Document Fracture Anemia:** A significant drop in hemoglobin due to fracture hematoma and surgical blood loss should be documented as **Acute Posthemorrhagic Anemia (D62)** (CC) if it requires monitoring, repeat labs, or transfusion.
> 3. **Specify Electrolyte Shifts:** Elderly patients often present with hyponatremia due to dehydration or chronic diuretic use. Document **Hyponatremia (E87.1)** (CC) when active IV saline replacement or free water restriction is initiated.

---

### ❤️ 3. Coronary Artery Bypass Graft (CABG)
* **Acuity Standing:** **Moderate Lag.** Huntsville Hospital captures MCCs in **41.13%** of bypass cases, lagging behind the national benchmark of **46.26%**. This leaves nearly **59%** of bypass patients in the baseline tier.
* **Clinical Insight:** CABG is a 2-tier DRG. Chronic underlying systemic and cardiac conditions must be documented with high specificity to capture the MCC.

> [!WARNING]
> **Surgeon Action Items for CABG:**
> 1. **Specify Myocardial Infarction Status:** If a patient is admitted with an active heart attack that leads to the bypass, document **NSTEMI (I21.4)** (MCC) or **STEMI (I21.0-I21.3)** (MCC). "Coronary artery disease" alone has no severity weight.
> 2. **Differentiate Heart Failure Acuity:** Do not write "CHF." Specify the type and acuity. Document **Acute on Chronic Systolic Heart Failure (I50.23)** or **Acute on Chronic Diastolic Heart Failure (I50.33)** (MCCs) when diuresis or inotropic support is actively adjusted.
> 3. **Capture Post-pump Renal Failure:** Contrast media or bypass pumps can cause acute kidney injury. If creatinine rises $\ge 3\times$ baseline or requires renal consult, document **Acute Kidney Failure with Tubular Necrosis (N17.0)** (MCC) rather than "renal insufficiency."

### 🧫 4. Major Bowel Procedure
* **Acuity Standing:** **Critical Gap (Priority 1).** Huntsville Hospital has a massive documentation gap here. The "None" severity rate is **37.58%**—**more than double** the national average of **16.81%**. Conversely, the MCC rate (**27.52%**) is far below the national benchmark (**35.47%**).
* **Clinical Insight:** Bowel resections are highly complex procedures frequently performed on cancer, perforation, or ischemic bowel patients. Having over **37%** of these patients billed in the lowest severity tier indicates severe under-documentation of abdominal and systemic complications.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Surgeon Action Items for Major Bowel Procedures:**
> 1. **Document Peritonitis and Perforations:** A bowel perforation causing peritonitis is a major clinical event. Ensure **Perforation of Intestine (K63.1)** (MCC) and **Acute Peritonitis (K65.0)** (MCC) are clearly written in the admission and operative notes.
> 2. **Capture Sepsis and Septic Shock:** If a patient presents with systemic inflammatory response (SIRS) due to an abdominal infection, document **Sepsis (A41.9)** (MCC). If they require vasopressors (e.g., Levophed) post-op, document **Severe Sepsis with Septic Shock (R65.21)** (MCC). Avoid terms like "urosepsis" or "bacteremia."
> 3. **Identify Post-op Ileus:** A common complication after bowel surgery is a prolonged ileus. Document **Postoperative Ileus (K91.89)** (CC) when it requires prolonged NPO, NG tube placement, or TPN.
> 4. **Acknowledge Malnutrition:** Patients with bowel obstructions or colon cancer often have chronic weight loss. Ensure the dietitian's assessment is reviewed. If severe, document **Severe Protein-Calorie Malnutrition (E43)** (MCC).

---

### 🧠 5. Spinal Fusion
* **Acuity Standing:** **Critical Gap (Priority 2).** Huntsville Hospital has a massive gap in spinal fusion. **56.99%** of patients are billed in the baseline "None" tier, compared to only **24.62%** nationally. Huntsville captured **0.00%** MCCs (due to low volume/suppression in high-severity DRGs, or absolute failure to capture).
* **Clinical Insight:** Spine fusion is a high-cost procedure where capturing underlying comorbidities makes a significant difference in reimbursement and resource alignment.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Surgeon Action Items for Spinal Fusion:**
> 1. **Specify Nicotine Dependence:** Nicotine is a major risk factor that compromises bone graft union. Document **Nicotine Dependence (F17.210)** (CC) in the patient's history. This is clinically vital and carries a CC weight.
> 2. **Document Chronic Opioid Use:** Patients undergoing fusion have often been on long-term pain management. Document **Opioid Dependence (F11.20)** (CC) rather than "history of narcotic use."
> 3. **Document Vertebral Infections:** If the fusion is performed to treat a spinal infection, document **Vertebral Osteomyelitis (M46.20)** (MCC) as the primary indication.
> 4. **Specify Neurogenic Complications:** If the patient has spinal stenosis causing spinal cord compression, document **Paraplegia (G82.20)** or **Quadriplegia (G82.50)** (MCCs) if active neurological deficits are present and being managed.

## 4. Strategic Recommendations for Huntsville Hospital Surgical Leadership

To successfully implement these changes, Huntsville Hospital should execute three clinical documentation workflow modifications:

1. **EHR Smart-Set Integration:** Add structured check-boxes or dropdown menus in post-operative order sets that prompt surgeons to specify the severity of common complications (e.g., prompting for AKI Stage 2/3 instead of "renal insufficiency" or "fluid overload").
2. **Targeted Colectomy & Spine Audits:** Establish a focused audit loop where CDI specialists review all major bowel procedures and spinal fusions coded as "Without CC/MCC" prior to billing release.
3. **Surgeon Peer-to-Peer Feedback:** Share these regional benchmarks directly in department meetings. Showing surgeons that their peers at other Alabama hospitals are capturing acuity at twice their rate is the most effective driver of documentation compliance.
